02 — Deliverable

A repository your CI can keep.

The work product is the suite. A PDF that cannot fail in CI becomes a souvenir the week a new commit reopens the bug.

  • Property table

    Each named property, the state it talks about, and the test that pins it. Written so an engineer who was not in the walkthrough can see what was claimed.

  • Tests on the money identity

    Invariant or fork tests aimed at the value that actually moves: shares, receipts, cursors, fees, journals, image ids. The handler is part of the delivery, not a private note.

  • A failing test when a property breaks

    If a named property does not hold, the repo contains the sequence that shows it. If every named property holds under the tests that ran, the table says that, and only that.

  • One walkthrough

    How the surface was read, which identity was treated as the money, and which paths were left outside the pack. Terms live in SERVICES.md.

03 — Method

Five days, in order.

The calendar is the shape of the pack. It is a review of the named surface. A green suite means those properties held under those tests.

  1. Day 0

    Confirm

    The surface is named in writing. I accept it or I send it back. Payment has not happened yet.

  2. Days 1–2

    Read the identity

    Where value is credited, where it is burned, and which second ledger, cursor, fee base, or journal field can disagree with the first.

  3. Day 3

    Write the break

    A concrete sequence: two exits on one deposit, a fee taken on principal, a share price moved by a donation, a seal accepted twice.

  4. Day 4

    Pin both sides

    The sequence stays as a test. Where the state space is the point, a handler drives it. The killed path and the holding path are different tests.

  5. Day 5

    Hand over

    Private repo, property table, one walkthrough. Your patch window opens the same day and runs for fourteen days.

04 — What the review is built to see

Three shapes that survive a clean diff.

Most of the expensive bugs in a single vault, a share pool, or a proof gate are not a mystery opcode. The protocol is sure about an identity the code does not enforce.

Vault

Two ledgers, one pile.

A deposit credits shares and a receipt. Each exit burns only one of them. The same shape shows up in queue cursors, fast and slow paths, and trusted forwarders. The fix keeps both exits and spends one balance. A withdrawal of 40 must leave 60 on the receipt, and the other depositor must still be paid.

Shares

The ledger balances while someone is paid from principal.

Inflation donations move a price that should have ignored them. A fee on the whole ledger, principal included, harvests depositors. Solvency can still hold. The bound is the fee against gain, and the dust bound has to be rate-relative. A flat wei ceiling is how a green suite lies.

zkVM

The proof is valid. The story is the wrong one.

The guest proves a computation. The journal commits what the application will believe. The verifier checks a seal against an image id. Replay of a stateless verifier is expected. A payment gate has to nullify the statement, and a longer seal must hit the same key. If the journal has no chain id, a second deployment is a different problem, and the table should say so.

05 — Evidence

Runnable, and not a disclosure.

Four public repositories. Teaching contracts and a public map. Clone them, run forge test, and watch a property fail on the broken variant and hold on the bound one. Nothing here is a finding against a live protocol.

01 split-ledger-vault-demo

100 deposited. 200 extracted.

The broken vault pays a deposit through shares and again through the receipt. The bound vault keeps both doors. Forty out through shares leaves sixty on the receipt. A second depositor is paid in full. Two actors, 768 calls, both invariants hold. Default suite: 19 passed, 1 gated suite skipped.

02 pool-invariant-suite-demo

Inflation and fee creep, both killed.

Two classes, two invariant suites on the fixed contracts. Donation does not move the price. The fee is taken on gain, capped, and the dust bound is rate-relative. Default suite: 24 passed, 3 skipped. The fork run and the two killed suites stay opt-in.

03 risc0-onchain-verify-demo

A real Groth16 seal. A gate that pays once.

Rust guest, committed receipt, Solidity verifier. Tampered journal and a wrong image id revert. The raw verifier accepts the same seal twice, and a longer seal still verifies. The payment gate nullifies the journal digest, so the second shape does not pay. The journal is 24 bytes and has no chain id. Default suite: 12 passed.

04 zkvm-guest-bug-notes

Twelve classes. Already-public sources.

A map of the guest and the binding layer: host input, executor and circuit split, journal binding, verification-key allowlists, replay, and ELF-to-key upgrades. Every reference was already public. No undisclosed bugs.

08 — Questions

Before you send the repo.

Is this a security audit?

It is a security review of one named surface. It is not a security audit, and it is not a guarantee. A green suite means the tested properties held under the tested conditions. Another reviewer, a contest, a bounty, and monitoring remain the right next steps for a launch.

What if the review finds nothing?

You still receive the property table, the tests, and the walkthrough. The fee is for the review of that surface. The table records what held, and it does not inflate a clean run into a finding.

What if my diff is larger than 800 lines, or I have two contracts?

That does not fit this pack. I confirm the fit before you pay. A second surface can be a second pack after the first repo is delivered. It is not folded into the first five days.

When do the five days start, and how do I pay?

You email the surface. I confirm it fits within one business day. You pay $1,500 by USDC or bank transfer. An invoice is available on request. The five business days start when the payment lands. No wallet address is published here.

Who owns the repository?

You do. The repo is private. It holds the tests, the property table, and the notes from the walkthrough. The public samples on this page are separate teaching code.

What are the four public repositories?

Teaching material. The vault, the pool, and the zkVM gate are constructed so a property fails and then holds. The notes map twelve already-public bug classes. They are not client work and not live-protocol disclosures.
